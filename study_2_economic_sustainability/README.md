# Study 2: Economic Development and Environmental Sustainability

**Status:** Environment design selected; updated reward comparison pending.

## Overview

This study builds on [Study 1](../study_1_emissions_coordination/README.md) by introducing economic development and resource management alongside pollution mitigation.

Each region wants to grow economically, but development also increases emissions. Regions need to decide whether to invest in their economy, spend resources on reducing pollution, or save currency for future decisions.

The challenge is that pollution doesn't stay within regional boundaries, and if pollution in any region reaches a critical level, the entire system fails.

I'm interested in exploring how independent agents balance their own economic growth with the environmental needs of the larger system, especially when their decisions affect neighboring regions.

## The Environment

The simulation has four neighboring regions, each controlled by an independent Q-learning agent.

Each agent can choose between five actions:

- **Save:** Keep currency for future decisions.
- **Develop:** Invest in economic growth, increasing income but also emissions.
- **Low, medium, or high mitigation:** Spend currency to prevent new emissions at different levels.

Agents observe their own Economic Development Index (EDI), available currency, and regional pollution, along with a shared Global Pollution Index (GPI).

Emissions increase with economic development using a nonlinear formula:

`Emissions = 8 + 0.10 × EDI + 0.001 × EDI²`

Mitigation prevents new emissions but doesn't remove pollution already accumulated. Remaining emissions contribute to regional pollution and can spill over to neighboring regions.

Each episode lasts up to 200 steps. If any region reaches a pollution level of 100, the entire system fails.

## Reward Structures

As in Study 1, I'll compare three reward structures:

- **Local:** Agents prioritize their own regional outcomes.
- **Global:** Agents consider the overall system.
- **Mixed:** Agents balance local and global objectives.

The current economic reward considers both immediate development gains and the additional income generated by development.

Unlike Study 1, agents also need to manage their currency. Development and mitigation have costs, so agents must decide when to spend and when to save.

The idea is to see how different reward structures influence economic development, pollution, and the agents' ability to avoid environmental failure.
